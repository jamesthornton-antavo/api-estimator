How the numbers are worked out ▾

Why not an annual average

Hosting is sized on the busiest minute, not on annual volume. Spreading a year of calls evenly across every minute understates the need by around two orders of magnitude, because retail traffic gathers into trading hours, into a handful of days, and into minutes inside those days.

One limit, every source

The requests-per-minute limit is shared by everything that calls Antavo with your key: tills, website and app. Sizing from the point of sale alone understates it.

The chain

Yearly events × calls per event × fan-out × retries gives yearly calls. Divided by trading days that gives an average day. A day multiplier gives a busy or peak day. Till calls are then spread across store opening hours and lifted to the busiest till hour; online calls take the busiest hour’s share of the day’s online traffic. Dividing by 60 gives requests per minute, and the burst factor gives the busiest minute. Channel overlap trims it, because tills and website rarely peak in the same minute.

What counts

Antavo counts every request that reaches it with your workspace’s key, from tills, website and app alike. Work the platform does internally after a request arrives is not counted, and neither are transactions queued through the Async Events API.

Sustained and peak limits

Each plan has a sustained limit and a higher peak limit. Peaks above the sustained limit are allowed on no more than 5 days in any 30, so the busy-day figure should sit under the sustained limit and only the busiest days should need the peak.
